Guiding questions
\begin{itemize}
    \item What is your original contribution? Why should the examiner care about your research?
    \item What is the thesis problem statement?
    \item What do you (not) hope to achieve?
    \item What are the research questions and hypotheses?
    \item What are your epistemological and ontological positions?
    \item What is your contribution to the field?
    \item How is the thesis laid out?
\end{itemize}

A typical PhD thesis introduction follows the following format:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Introduction to the introduction: a short version (of only a few paragraphs) of the thesis' aims, research questions, contribution, objectives and findings.
    \item State the overarching topic and aims of the thesis in more detail
    \item Provide a brief review of the literature related to the topic (this will be very brief if you have a separate literature review chapter)
    \item Define the terms and scope of the topic
    \item Critically evaluate the current state of the literature on that topic and identify your gap
    \item Outline why the research is important and the contribution that it makes
    \item Outline your epistemological and ontological position
    \item Clearly outline the research questions and problem(s) you seek to address
    \item State the hypotheses (if you are using any)
    \item Detail the most important concepts and variables
    \item Briefly describe your methodology
    \item Discuss the main findings
    \item Discuss the layout of the thesis
\end{itemize}

Once you have finished your thesis, ask yourself the following questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Does the first line of the introduction discuss the problem that your thesis is addressing and the contribution that it is making?
    \item Does the introduction provide an overview of the thesis and end with a brief discussion on the content of each chapter?
    \item Does the introduction make a case for the research?
    \item Have the research questions/problems/hypotheses been clearly outlined (preferably early on)?
\end{itemize}
