\section{Work overview}
    \debug{%
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Introduction to the introduction: a short version (of only a few paragraphs) of the thesis' aims, research questions, contribution, objectives and findings.
    \end{itemize}
    }

    The economic success of oil and gas fields is increasingly contingent on the quality of reservoir management decisions, 
    which are made under uncertainty and based on limited data.
    %
    Kalman-based ensemble methods 
    \cite{Evensen:1994, Evensen:1996, Emerick:2013, Skjervheim:2011}
    are widely used to quantify uncertainty in reservoir forecasts by adjusting reservoir models to match production data, 
    but require vast computational resources to perform repeated reservoir simulations.
    %
    In an effort to reduce the computational burden, Data-Space Inversion (DSI) 
    \cite{Sun:2017}
    was introduced as an alternative to traditional history matching by updating ensemble forecasts directly in data space, 
    thereby avoiding repeated subsurface simulations.
    
    \citeonline{Lima:2020} introduced DSI-ESMDA, an extension of DSI that employs the 
    Ensemble Smoother with Multiple Data Assimilation (ES-MDA) 
    \cite{Emerick:2013}
    to replace the Randomized Maximum Likelihood (RML) minimizations of the original formulation with an iterative Kalman-type update, 
    achieving significant computational efficiency while maintaining competitive posterior forecasts.
    However, DSI-ESMDA, like related ensemble smoothers, remains susceptible to excessive variance loss, 
    which can lead to underestimation of posterior uncertainty.
    
    This work introduces an extension to DSI-ESMDA that modifies the effective influence of each observation through a
    nonlinear, misfit-dependent weighting of the innovation vector.
    %
    The proposed formulation can be interpreted as introducing a smoothing influence function into the Kalman-type update,
    computed from normalized innovations using an error-function-based gating operator.
    %
    Findings on both synthetic and real reservoirs show that Gated DSI-ESMDA reduces variance loss during 
    and produces more robust uncertainty envelopes in the prediction phase without degrading the data match.
    
    Gated DSI-ESMDA acts as a stabilizing extension rather than a replacement, 
    and can be applied to standard ensemble-based model-space inversion methods, including ES-MDA and related iterative smoothers.
    The power of this contribution is that it provides a simple yet effective mean of improving the robustness of ensemble-based inversion methods 
    without requiring additional simulations or complex modifications to existing workflows.

    \subsection{What is oil?}
    In the context of reservoir engineering, oil refers to the more precise term petroleum, 
    a combination of the latin \emph{petra} (rock) and \emph{oleum} (oil in the general sense).
    %
    Petroleum is an umbrella term for complex mixtures resulting from the deposition and decomposition 
    of organic matter during a long period of time.
    %
    It is composed of hundreds of chemical components, 
    with the main constituent being hydrocarbons, 
    i.e., organic compounds primarily composed of carbon and hydrogen atoms.
    
    In its liquid phase, petroleum is inflammable, less dense than water, has a characteristic smell,
    and a color anywhere between black and light-brown.
    %
    Petroleum is extracted from geological reservoirs, and its components, 
    especially the hydrocarbons, used in applications that range from 
    paving roads, to making plastic-based constructs, to fueling airplanes and cars, 
    turning oil production into an extremely profitable economic activity.      

    \subsection{What is a geological reservoir?}
    Geological reservoirs are subsurface formations capable of storing and trans-
    mitting fluids, such as hydrocarbons (oil and gas), water, hydrogen,
    carbon dioxide, and waste materials from industrial processes
    \cite[section 1.1]{Emerick:2025}. 

    In order for oil to exist within a reservoir, 
    it must occupy empty spaces within appropriate rock formations.
    %
    The amount of empty space a rock has is called porosity, 
    since it comes in the form of small pores in the material.
    %
    How much of that empty space is actually occupied is called saturation, and changes in time.
    %
    Importantly, in order to oil to be extractable from inside the reservoir, 
    these pores filled with oil must be sufficiently connected,
    allowing the fluid to come through it.
    The ease through which a fluid can flow through a porous media is called permeability.

    In conventional petroleum reservoirs,
    oil is stored inside relatively porous and permeable rock formations 
    that are trapped by much less permeable rock formations or geological faults.
    %
    In contrast, unconventional reservoirs have the oil in porous rocks 
    so impermeable the sealing property of a cap rock is unneeded.    

    If there is economic interest in a petroleum reservoir, 
    i.e., it is sufficiently large (possibly by linking with other reservoirs),
    it can be referred to in the industry as an oil field. 
    In this work, the terms reservoir, oil field, or simply field, are used interchangeably.
    %
    When oil production is no longer economically feasible, the field is either abandoned or repurposed.
    This is the end of the reservoir's life, which started when wells were first completed. 

    \subsection{The inverse problem}
    \label{subsec:inverse-problem}
    Oil production has been established as an extremely profitable, but extremely difficult economic activity.
    The scarcity of this natural resource pushes the search for new reservoirs offshore 
    and in increasingly unfavorable geological formations, 
    of which the discovery and extraction of oil from the Brazilian pre-salt is the prime example.
    %
    As extraction becomes less profitable, strategies for oil production enhancement become attractive.
    %
    This usually means closing (at least partially) producer wells, 
    or injecting fluids into the reservoir 
    (water and gas being the most common in this context) 
    through injector wells in order to favorably manipulate the permeability and/or pressure within the filed,
    whose aging means the plunging of pressure and oil saturation.

    Controlling the reservoir often requires knowledge and prediction of subsurface dynamics and rock properties.
    %
    More specifically, 
    given the observations coming from an oil field (e.g., oil production total, pressure at a wellhead),
    and other geological information on the reservoir (e.g., porosity and permeability of a rock sample),
    it is required to find the underlying parameters and states of the reservoir. 
    %
    Parameters are physical properties of the reservoir that remain constant throughout its life,
    their main representatives being rock permeability and porosity. 
    %
    Conversely, states are physical properties that vary throughout reservoir life,
    their main representatives being saturation and pressure
    \cite[section 4.1]{Emerick:2025}.
    %
    Once identified, parameters and states can be used to construct a model of the reservoir, 
    which in turn can be deployed to generate predictions of its reference.

    Processing data-space outputs of the field into estimates of model-space parameters is referred to 
    as the inverse problem in this work.
    %
    As the following subsection shows, the inverse problem is ill-posed in practice, 
    and while great progress has been done in improving subsurface simulations and the data inversion process,
    repeated model simulations remain a cost to be mitigated.

\section{Research problem}

        \item Outline your epistemological and ontological position

    Literature on DSI and DSI-ESMDA take different approaches to mitigating variance loss, but they are either computationally expensive or require careful tuning of hyperparameters.
    Ideally, a solution would be simple to implement, require minimal tuning, and be computationally efficient.
    This work proposes introducing a straightforward gating mechanism on DSI-ESMDA's innovation vector, which selectively suppresses the influence of well-matched observations while preserving the impact of significant mismatches.
    Gated DSI-ESMDA thus modulates the posterior ensemble variance with a single parameter, the intensity of the gating function.

    This work addresses the following research questions:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Can a gating mechanism be introduced into DSI-ESMDA to selectively modulate the influence of observations based on their match quality?
        \item Does the proposed innovation gating reduce variance loss during assimilation and produce more robust uncertainty envelopes in the prediction phase without degrading the data match?
        \item How does the performance of Gated DSI-ESMDA compare to standard DSI-ESMDA across synthetic and real reservoir case studies?
    \end{enumerate}

\section{Significance}
    \debug{%
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Restate the importance of the research in more detail
    \end{itemize}
    }

    This work introduces an extension to DSI-ESMDA that modifies the effective influence of each observation 
    through a nonlinear, misfit-dependent weighting of the innovation vector.
    %
    The proposed formulation can be interpreted as introducing a smoothing influence function into the Kalman-type update, 
    computed from normalized innovations using an error-function-based gating operator.
    %
    In contrast to covariance localization, which acts on second-order statistics, innovation gating acts directly on the update term, 
    selectively suppressing corrections associated with sufficiently-matched observations
    while preserving the full impact of significant mismatches.
    %
    Match quality is thresholded by a gating parameter during innovation normalization.
    %
    The gating methodology is inspired by adaptive filtering developed to mitigate actuator wear in closed-loop control systems 
    \cite{Fortaleza:2022} 
    and is conceptually related to smooth shrinkage and noise-gating strategies in signal processing 
    \cite{Donoho:1994,Donoho:1995}.
    %
    Although the present work focuses on DSI, the proposed formulation 
    is general and can be applied to standard ensemble-based model-space inversion methods, 
    including ES-MDA and related iterative smoothers.
    %
    The proposed method inherits the computational efficiency of DSI-ESMDA while providing a robust, 
    easily tunable mechanism for modulating posterior distribution variance,
    setting it as a practical and effective tool for uncertainty quantification in reservoir forecasting.
    
\section{Methodology overview}
    \debug{%
    \begin{itemize}
        \item State the hypotheses (if you are using any)

        \item Briefly describe your methodology
        \item Discuss the main findings
    \end{itemize}

    The approach is evaluated against the original DSI-ESMDA on two synthetic reservoirs 
    and one real field application using measurements from producers and injectors.
    %
    The synthetic reservoirs are the Egg 
    \cite{Jansen:2014}
    and Olympus 
    \cite{Fonseca:2020}
    ensembles, which are widely used in the literature to 
    benchmark history matching and uncertainty quantification methods,
    and the real field application is a mature oil field in the Brazilian Post-Salt.
    %
    Posterior ensembles are compared across several metrics, 
    including observation coverage and mean squared error
    \cite[subsection 9.4.2]{Emerick:2025}, 
    to assess the impact of innovation gating on variance loss and uncertainty quantification.
    %
    Results show that innovation-gated DSI-ESMDA reduces variance loss during and produces 
    more robust uncertainty envelopes in the prediction phase without significantly degrading the data match.
    %
    Furthermore, for moderate gating parameters, posterior forecasts remain close to those obtained with standard DSI-ESMDA, 
    indicating that the proposed method acts as a stabilizing extension rather than a replacement.
