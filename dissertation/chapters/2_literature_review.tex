\chapter{Literature review}
\label{chap:literature-review}

\chapteroutline{%
    This section provides a brief literature review of these methods, which are split in two major groups:
    model-space methods (\cref{sec:model-space-methods}) and 
    data-space methods (\cref{sec:data-space-methods}).
    %
    Details are kept as short as possible, specially in \cref{sec:model-space-methods},
    and mathematical formulations omitted.
}

Most reservoir prediction methods are conceptually linked to the Kalman Filter (KF)
\cite{Kalman:1960} 
as they attempt to, through the Bayesian theorem, improve a probability distribution given observations of the reservoir
using linear update(s). 
%
The distribution \emph{a priori} to data assimilation is called the prior distribution,
and represents the belief one holds for the expected distribution.
Conversely, the distribution \emph{a posteriori} to data assimilation is called the posterior distribution.

The main difference between a model-space method and a data-space method is the distribution being updated.
Model-space methods apply the Kalman-like update to an ensemble of model realizations, targeting distributions of model parameters.
Subsurface simulations of the adjusted models are then performed to obtain predictions on reservoir states and flow rates.
% 
Data-space methods apply the Kalman-like update to an ensemble of predictions, targeting distributions of reservoir states and flow rates directly.
Compared to model-space methods, the number of subsurface simulations is greatly reduced (only being required to establish the initial distribution),
but do not offer insights to causes to reservoir behavior.

At the extreme, machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) methods can be applied directly to the ensemble of predictions,
resulting in a posterior distribution not conceptually related to the Kalman update.
%
Among model-space approaches, DL is also becoming increasingly popular due to its ability to estimate a model's simulation outputs,
effectively proxying the simulator and consequently speeding up the history matching process
(e.g., \citeonline{Xiao:2024, Tao:2025}).

Ultimately, a reservoir prediction method is concerned with bringing the posterior of predictions 
as close as possible to reservoir observations without underestimating uncertainty.

\section{Model-space methods}
\label{sec:model-space-methods}

\subsection{Ensemble Kalman Filter}

The cradle of modern model-space methods is the Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF)
introduced by \citeonline{Evensen:1994}, which in turn is an adaptation of KF.

KF is an optimal estimator for variables of a linear system \cite[section 4.1]{Emerick:2025}
that updates its estimation iteratively with each data assimilation.
%
The result of a data assimilation step is the mean and covariance of the target variables posterior distribution.
%
Its update equation, which can be derived from Bayes' theorem, 
consists of adding a correction to the mean.
%
This correction is proportional to the difference
between observed data and the prior distribution, commonly referred to as innovation.
%
The proportionality constant, referred to as the Kalman gain, 
depends on the noise in observations as well as the prior's covariance%
\footnote{%
    The posterior of an assimilation becomes the prior for the next assimilation,
    updating the Kalman gain.
}.

In the context of history matching, 
the system is a reservoir, 
the variables being estimated are model parameters,
and the system outputs are reservoir states and flow rates.
%
KF can't be directly applied to the inverse problem on acocunt of 
(i) the nonlinear relationship between system variables and outputs, and 
(ii) the size of the posterior covariance (trillions of entries if finer gridblocks are used)%
\footnote{%
    Knowledge on measurement noise is also a limitation of KF, albeit not particular to reservoir prediction.
}.

The first limitation can be addressed by running simulations of the reservoir model, 
substituting the linear relationship with a nonlinear function.
EnKF addresses the second limitations by deploying an ensemble of model realizations. 
%
Instead of updating a single vector of model parameters, 
EnKF updates each paramater vector in the ensemble, approximating the prior covariance with the prior ensemble. 
%
Since the number of realizations is usually much smaller than the number of parameters (hundreds versus millions),
this approximation is computationally feasible.

More precisely, EnKF estimates model parameters and reservoir states simultaneously, 
and its current formulation also applies perturbations to innovations in order to avoid underestimating uncertainty 
\cite{Burgers:1998,Houtekamer:1998}.
%
While EnKF has been a influential tool for reservoir prediction \cite{Aanonsen:2009, Oliver:2011}, 
specially after the work of \citeonline{Geir:2002},
its framework is general and has been successfully applied in many fields, such as
oceanography \cite{Bertino:2003, Keppenne:2003}, 
atmospheric modeling \cite{Whitaker:2008,Houtekamer:2016},
numerical weather prediction \cite{Houtekamer:2005:Monthly,Houtekamer:2005:Quaterly,Szunyogh:2005,Otsuka:2016}, 
and hydrology \cite{Reichle:2002, Chen:2006, Liu:2008, Sun:2016}.

\subsection{Iterative Ensemble Smoother}

In the EnKF framework, data assimilations occur one time step at a time,
a process referred to as Sequential Data Assimilation (SDA).
%
For systems with complicated relationships between estimated variables and system outputs,
SDA means spending a great deal of time extracting these outputs.

This is case of geological reservoirs.
Furthermore, EnKF estimates reservoir states and model parameters jointly,
and might produce inconsistent reservoir states estimates.
% 
The Ensemble Smoother (ES) tackles these limitations by substituting SDA 
with a single data assimilation that considers all data points at once to obtain a posterior of model parameters.

ES was introduced by \citeonline{Leeuwen:1996}, 
and \citeonline{Skjervheim:2011} were the first to apply it to reservoir models.
Implementation wise, ES is effectively a single EnKF update on each model realization 
that uses all available observations at once.

Their success spurred researchers to develop similarly batching assimilations.
Particularly, it was seen that the single ES update often produced data matches of insufficient quality,
which lead to the development of iterative formulations of ES (IES).
%
IES methods run the model update rule until convergence conditions have been satisfied, 
or a given number of iterations has been met.
%
It is important to note that while IES iterations do require reservoir simulations, 
the number of iterations is usually much smaller than the number of time steps,
which effectively guarantees computational time will be smaller than EnKF approaches.

Ensemble RML \cite{Chen:2012}, 
and its subsequent Levenberg-Marquardt \cite{Chen:2013}
were some of the first IES algorithms to be formulated.
%
Since then, IES has remained an active research and study topic in history matching literature
\cite{Luo:2015, Zhang:2018, Raanes:2019, Maschio:2024}.
For more details on IES methods, the reader is referred to 
\citeonline{Evensen:2018, Evensen:2023}.

\subsection{Ensemble Smoother with Multiple Data Assimilation}

Of the proposed IES methods, the most influential is perhaps the 
Ensemble Smoother with Multiple Data Assimilation (ESMDA) 
introduced by \citeonline{Emerick:2013:CompGeosci, Emerick:2013:SPE}.

The Multiple Data Assimilation (MDA) mechanism was 
first developed to improve EnKF data assimilations
\cite{Emerick:2012}.
%
In a word, MDA repeats the data assimilation step, but with perturbed observations.
The covariance of the perturbation is inflated so that the greater the number of repetitions,
the greater the covariance%
\footnote{%
    As such, ESMDA with a single MDA iteration reduces to ES.
}

By doing so, MDA improves the conditioning to data while usually avoiding underestimating uncertainty,
specially in nonlinear model-data relationships.
%
Therefore, ESMDA enjoys the computational speed of ES while also offering a 
straightforward mechanism through which one can mitigate the drawbacks of ES.
%
However, MDA inflation coefficients and number of iterations must be defined in advance,
and finding their optimal values remain open research topics
\cite[subsection 5.3.5]{Emerick:2025}.

ESMDA's track record and simplicity of implementation make it
a solid history matching algorithm that is 
amply employed both in industry and in research
\cite{Grana:2019, Neto:2021, Tao:2025}.

\section{Data-space methods}
\label{sec:data-space-methods} 

\subsection{Prediction Focused Analysis}
    Data-space methods for reservoir prediction are relatively new in literature, 
    but have been gaining momentum with the Artificial Intelligence boom.
    %
    The first algorithm proposed in this context capable of uncertainty quantification
    is due to \citeonline{Scheidt:2015},
    who referred to the proposed method as Prediction Focused Analysis (PFA).
    
    PFA uses low-dimensional representations of the ensemble prior in order to 
    approximate conditional probability distributions to field observations.
    %
    The approximated distribution is then projected back to the original dimension, 
    allowing for predictions with quantification of uncertainty.
    
    Since projections come from a bijective, smooth, nonlinear mapping, 
    points close to the observations remain close after dimensionality reduction.
    %
    This allows PFA to also serve as a tool for dignosing ensemble quality, 
    given that ensembles representative of data would clutter 
    more around observed data in the low-dimension space.   

    However, PFA scales poorly with data dimensionality,
    and while changes would be made to the mapping process
    \cite{Satija:2015, Satija:2017},
    the method saw limited application in reservoir prediction.

\subsection{Data Space Inversion}
    \citeonline{Sun:2017:A} introduce the Data-Space Inversion (DSI) algorithm, 
    the first data-space method to allow for uncertainty quantification 
    while also scalinng reasonably with data dimension. 

    Similarly to PFA, DSI obtains a posterior distribution of data by sampling from a lower-dimensional space.
    While the mapping remains nonlinear, smooth and bijective, 
    the conceptual difference from PFA is that the parameterization vector (or latent vector)
    is a random vector with fixed probability distribution.

    For each realization in the prior ensemble, 
    this vector is sampled and the mapping inverted in order to 
    project the posterior back to the original data space.
    %
    The sampling is defined by an optimization step known as Randomized Maximum Likelihood (RML).

    DSI was shown to work adequately with reservoir forecasting since its conception
    \cite{Sun:2017:A, Sun:2017:B},
    and was also applied to carbon storage shortly after
    \cite{Sun:2019}.
    
    By extending the prior vectors with well controls, 
    \citeonline{Jiang:2020} managed to adapt the DSI framework beyond prediction and into closed-loop control.
    %
    This approach, named Data-Space Inversion with Variable Controls (DSIVC),
    would be revisited by
    \citeonline{Liu:2023},
    who coupled the connection element method (CEM) 
    in order to improve subsurface simulation and models with complex geological formation. 

    \citeonline{Liu:2021} improve DSI by solving the RML optimization with
    a stochastic approximation of the objective function gradient's, 
    which lead to excelent predictions of reservoir states.
    %
    \citeonline{Zhan:2024} would later employ the version of DSI that uses this stochastic approach to RML
    as a proxy model to their proposed worflow of history matching horizontal wells.  
    
    \citeonline{Beer:2025} decomposed the DSI mapping 
    in a way that makes sampling the posterior direct, avoiding the RML optimization. 
    Their work has shown promising results, but lack applications on non-synthetic reservoirs.
    
\subsection{Data Space Inversion with ESMDA}
    DSI's biggest bottleneck is the sampling of the latent variable, which is usually done with RML. 
    In a method referred to as Data Space Inversion with ESMDA (DSI-ESMDA)%
    \footnote{%
        In this abbreviation, DSI refers to the fact that the method acts directly on the data space.
        DSI-ESMDA and the DSI proposed by \cite{Sun:2017:A} are not conceptually linked.
    },
    \citeonline{Lima:2020} apply the ESMDA framework directly to data space, 
    doing away with the requirements of RML.
    %
    Since MDA iterations are much faster to compute, DSI-ESMDA enjoys faster performance than DSI.
    DSI-ESMDA's posteriors were seen to be competitive against, if not better than,
    the posteriors given by DSI and ESMDA.
    %
    The use of a model-space framework applied directly to data space had profound effects on data-space methods. 

    Shortly after, \citeonline{Jiang:2021:CompSci} would reformulate the original DSI approach in two fronts:
    first, DL algorithms would be used to compute the prior ensemble of latent variables;
    second, the ensemble of latent variables would be updated using ESMDA,
    or more precisely, the ESMDA rule applied to data space.
    %
    The reformulated DSI, referred in this work as DSI with DL,
    presented good results on synthetic case studies, 
    and was later put to the test on a complex real field 
    \cite{Jiang:2021:Frontiers}, with good results.
    %
    \citeonline{Jiang:2021:Hydrology} introduced a new ensemble of latent variables 
    to quantify model uncertainty in the distribution of data, 
    presenting excelent results in synthetic reservoir case studies.
    
    \citeonline{Jiang:2024} refined the DL procedure used for finding the latent variables in DSI with DL,
    lending the method better for forecasting of reservoir states (e.g., field's pressure and saturation).
    %
    \citeonline{He:2026} expands the range of applications of DSI by deploying the aforementioned refined framework 
    to forecast pressure, strain and stress fields in carbon storage problems.

    In an approach similar to DSIVC,
    \citeonline{Szklarz:2022} 
    apply DSI with DL on two synthetic problems:
    first, forecasting of carbon storage; 
    second, optimization of water injection rates of a hydrocarbon reservoir.
    %
    The authors 
    would later broaden the application of DSI with DL to 
    optimize well placement in a realistic hydrocarbon reservoir
    \cite{Barros:2025}. 
    %
    Parallelly, \citeonline{Singh:2025} apply DSI with DL to optimize water injection rates of an oil field.

    \citeonline{Abbate:2022} use DSI-ESMDA as a basis for proposing a new method in forecasting carbon storage.
    The authors develop an IES tailor-made for this problem, and results were comparable to standard model-space approaches. 
    
    \citeonline{Lin:2023} compare the performance of DSI-ESMDA against 
    the original DSI algorithm and a logarithmic fit to data \cite{Duong:2010}
    in the forecast of two shale reservoir case studies.
    %
    DSI-ESMDA presented both high robustness and high computational efficiency 
    when compared to the other methods, validating the algorithm. 

    Some of these authors would move on to swap the Kalman-like gain in the DSI-ESMDA update 
    for a DL algorithm, making the update nonlinear with respect to the innovation vector
    \citeonline{Xiao:2023:GeoSci:224}%
    \footnote{%
        Confunsingly enough, authors also called this approach ``DSI with DL''.
    }.
    %
    The proposed reformulation was teste on a few synthetic case studies of carbon storage problems, with good results.

    \citeonline{Xiao:2023:Computers} expanded their approach to develop a shale reservoir control strategy.
    The proposed approach, named DSI with varying refracturing (DSIVRF),
    uses a DSI-ESMDA-like rule to obtain a posterior ensemble of reservoir predictions,
    and optimizes controls based on estimates of reservoir pressure. 
    %
    To avoid subsurface simulations, a DL algorithm is developed to approximate pressure distributions.  
    
\subsection{Machine Learning and Deep Learning}
    By now, it should have been clear to the reader that data-space is fertile soil for the development and application
    of ML and DL methods.
    %
    While this review has so far presented them as auxiliary tools for DSI and DSI-ESMDA, 
    the direct application of ML/DL algorithms is also possible.
    %
    In this case, the posterior is not obtained via an iterative update, as was the case with DSI-ESMDA,
    nor does it require RML sampling, as was the case with the original DSI,
    but via the inference process of a deep neural network.
    
    Detailing ML/DL algorithms is beyond the scope of this dissertation,
    but for examples of works in this line, the reader is referred to
    \citeonline{Xiao:2022},
    \citeonline{Xiao:2023:GeoSci:230}, and
    \citeonline{Kim:2025}.

    This chapter has presented an overview of model-space and data-space methods for reservoir prediction,
    with a slight focus to data-space methods.
    %
    Model-space methods are conceptually linked to the Kalman Filter,
    and developments in this area have historically focused on speeding up the data assimilation process.
    The state of the art in model-space methods are iterative ensemble smoothers, 
    of which ESMDA is the most influential algorithm.
    Machine and deep learning algorithms are also often deployed as proxy models in this context.
    %
    Data-space methods vary in their conceptual origins, but dimention-reduction tools are widely utilized.
    %
    The state of the art in data-space methods consists of a synergy between 
    deep learning algorithms for nonlinear estimation of an ensemble of latent variables, 
    and ESMDA-like iterations to update this ensemble.
