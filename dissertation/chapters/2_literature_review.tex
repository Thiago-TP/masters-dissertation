\debug{%
According to Hart (2018), the job of a literature review is to show five things 
(if you're using our free PhD Writing Template, you may recognise these):
\begin{itemize}
    \item What has been written on your topic
    \item Who the key authors are and what the key works are
    \item The main theories and hypotheses
    \item The main themes that exist in the literature
    \item Gaps and weaknesses that your study will then help fill
\end{itemize}
}

\debug{%
A literature review has three objectives:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Summarise what has already been discussed in your field, both to demonstrate that you understand your field and to show how your study relates to it.
    \item Highlight gaps, problems or shortcomings in existing research to show the original contribution that your thesis makes.
    \item Identify important studies, theories, methods or theoretical frameworks that can be applied in your research.
\end{itemize}
}

\debug{%
There are nine steps involved in conducting a literature review:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pick a broad topic
    \item Find the way in
    \item Who's saying what and when
    \item Take notes
    \item Narrow down the field
    \item Narrow down the sources
    \item Snowball
    \item Think about questions that haven't been asked
    \item Write early, write quickly and write relevantly
\end{itemize}
}

\section{History Matching}

Subsurface simulations are computationally expensive, 
many methods in the literature have been proposed to avoid the adjustment loop by directly adjusting the model production curves to match past data, 
without updating model parameters.
%
Notably, this does not do away with the need for a model, since the model is still used to generate the initial ensemble of production curves,
so such methods are not to be taken as substitutes of standard model-based approaches, but rather as complementary techniques.

\section{Data-Space Inversion}

A Data-Space Inversion (DSI) algorithm tackles the data matching problem 
by directly updating an ensemble of forecasts, doing away with the update of model parameters.

Originally, DSI used RML for sampling
\cite{Sun:2017},
requiring the solving of several minimization problems. 
%
DSI with Ensemble Smoother Multiple Data Assimilation (DSI-ESMDA)
\cite{Lima:2020}
speeds up the original DSI process by swapping the RML by iterative, Kalman-like updates to each member of the ensemble.
%
Recently, deep learning techniques have been used to further enhance DSI-ESMDA, with good results 
\cite{Jiang:2021,Xiao:2023,Jiang:2024}.

However, DSI-ESMDA may still make poor predictions despite good fit to past data.
%
In the language of Bayesian statistics, the starting ensemble data are samplings of the prior distribution of reservoir production,
and the ensemble data after DSI updates are samplings of the posterior distribution of reservoir production.
%
In that sense, poor results beyond the history period means that the ensemble posterior narrows around a curve that is not representative of the reference reservoir.

While the inherent problem of history matching may be impossible to solve, it may be mitigated by allowing the posterior to spread more.
%
In this paper, we propose a version of DSI-ESMDA that introduces variance in the posterior ensemble by 
adaptively and smoothly diminishing the effect of an iteration update the smaller the matching error.
%
This preserves the benefits of DSI-ESMDA, such as physics-driven localization and efficient execution,
while equipping it with an easy to implement, statistically based mechanism for posterior ensemble adjustment that is controlled by a single, easy-to interpret hyperparameter.
