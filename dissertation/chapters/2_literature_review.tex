According to Hart (2018), the job of a literature review is to show five things (if you’re using our free PhD Writing Template, you may recognise these):

\begin{itemize}
    \item What has been written on your topic
    \item Who the key authors are and what the key works are
    \item The main theories and hypotheses
    \item The main themes that exist in the literature
    \item Gaps and weaknesses that your study will then help fill
\end{itemize}
%
A literature review has three objectives:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Summarise what has already been discussed in your field, both to demonstrate that you understand your field and to show how your study relates to it.
    \item Highlight gaps, problems or shortcomings in existing research to show the original contribution that your thesis makes.
    \item Identify important studies, theories, methods or theoretical frameworks that can be applied in your research.
\end{itemize}

There are nine steps involved in conducting a literature review:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pick a broad topic
    \item Find the way in
    \item Who's saying what and when
    \item Take notes
    \item Narrow down the field
    \item Narrow down the sources
    \item Snowball
    \item Think about questions that haven’t been asked
    \item Write early, write quickly and write relevantly
\end{itemize}
