\section{Summary of findings}	
    \debug{Briefly restate key results}	
    This work introduces Gated DSI-ESMDA, an extension of DSI-ESMDA 
    with a nonlinear innovation gating mechanism that increases posterior variance 
    the more intense the gating.

    The proposed algorithm was evaluated on two synthetic reservoir ensembles, 
    Egg and Olympus, and on a real field in the Brazilian post-salt.
    %
    As expected, observation coverages of the posterior increased with the gating intensity for all case studies,
    albeit under different sensitivities.
    %
    Results indicate that Gated DSI-ESMDA could do away with observation data resampling as long as gating intensity is not too small,
    since for mild intensities posteriors are similar to, or better than, the posteriors produced by the standard DSI-ESMDA,
    at least on the metrics considered.
    
    Despite good results, Gated DSI-ESMDA is not beyond the limitations of its originator.
    %
    In the real field case, it was exposed that both DSI-ESMDA and Gated DSI-ESMDA 
    are susceptible to poor feature selection and localization tuning,
    as well as insuficient ground-truth representativeness, 
    high data dimentionality, and reduced ensemble size. 

\section{Contribution to knowledge}	
    \debug{State what is new and original}
    This work's novelty lies in the nonlinear innovation gating mechanism for posterior variance increase,
    which is a direct adaptation of the work of \citeonline{Fortaleza:2022}.

\section{Practical implications}	
    \debug{How findings apply in practice}
    By increasing or decreasing a single positive number, named the gating intensity,
    practitioners can straightforwardly adjust reservoir predictions.
    %
    As discussed in \cref{chap:discussion}, gating adjustment can be tuned as to match
    DSI-ESMDA outputs.
    %
    Practitioners can find and use these values as baselines for data assimilation procedures,
    increasing or decreasing intensity as they see fit.
    
    More generally, the gating mechanism in of itself can be easily adapted into model-space methods such as ES-MDA,
    acting on outputs of the forward model in order to produce more tempered model parameter estimates,
    just as it does with predictions in data-space.


\section{Limitations and future research}
\debug{%
\begin{itemize}
    \item Acknowledgment of Study Limitations
    \item Suggestions for Future Research
\end{itemize}
}
As an extension of DSI-ESMDA, Gated DSI-ESMDA shares the same benefits and limitations.
Feature selection and localization optimization are thought to be two of the main culprits behind
the lukewarm results in the real field case, and future research could address these problems.

\section{Closing statement}
    The research questions asked in \cref{sec:research-questions} are answered as follows. 
    
    \medskip

    \noindent{\itshape Can a gating mechanism be introduced into DSI-ESMDA to selectively modulate the influence of observations based on their match quality?}
    
    Yes, and successfully so. However, how much match quality can be feasibly improved is dependent on parameters within and beyond the proposed approach,
    such as gating intensity tuning, localization tuning, and feature selection. 

    \medskip

    \noindent{\itshape Does the proposed innovation gating reduce variance loss during assimilation and produce more robust uncertainty envelopes in the prediction phase without degrading the data match?}
    
    Yes. Posterior variance was shown to increase with gating intensity regardless of case study, but this does come at the cost of increased MSE and data-mismatch. 
    Informally speaking, the posterior does ``looser'' for it, and how much variance should be tolerated change between applications. 

    \medskip
        
    \noindent{\itshape How does the performance of Gated DSI-ESMDA compare to standard DSI-ESMDA across synthetic and real reservoir case studies?}
    
    For too small a gating intensity, the proposed approach tended to perform slightly worse,
    but mild gating produced similar results to DSI-ESMDA.

    \medskip

    In short, while this work has plenty of room for improvement and exploration, 
    it can be said to have been successful, and future efforts will be concentrated in improving it.
