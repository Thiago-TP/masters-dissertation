p.42
Ensemble-based methods encompass a range of data assimilation techniques
grounded in Monte Carlo formulations of the Kalman filter [228, 229].
The ensemble Kalman filter (EnKF), introduced by Evensen [136], was
the pioneering approach in this field. EnKF and its numerous variations
have achieved notable success across various domains, including oceanog-
raphy [33], atmospheric modeling [464], and numerical weather prediction
[201, 338].
The introduction of EnKF as a reservoir data assimilation method by
Nævdal et al. [313] sparked significant research activity, leading to the pub-
lication of numerous studies in subsequent years; see, e.g., [3, 331] and refer-
ences therein. In its standard form, EnKF uses an ensemble of states of the
dynamical system to represent the mean and covariance, which are updated
sequentially over time.

p.42-43
An alternative approach is to apply the EnKF equations to update the en-
tire history of observations simultaneously, a process known as the ensemble
smoother (ES) [448, 400]. The batch update scheme of ES makes the method
more appealing for reservoir applications, though it requires iterative strate-
gies to ensure a reasonable match of the observations. This fact led to the
development of several iterative forms of ES [77, 78, 132, 413, 279, 354].

This chapter reviews two popular versions of iterative ES: the ensemble
RML (EnRML) [77, 78] and the subspace iterative ES (SEIS) [354, 142].
Other iterative forms of ES exist in the literature but are not discussed here.
