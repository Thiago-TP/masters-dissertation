\section{Gated DSI-ESMDA}

A well tuned localization tends to greatly improve the quality of the posterior, 
but if the prior zeroes in on an average that is far mismatched from the reference,
it is hard to tune it in order to inject some variance on the collapsed curves.
This is partly the reason behind the random disturbance in injecting variance on the ensemble fit.

Even when the localization $\mathbf{R}$ is well tuned (i.e., $\theta$, $L_{x'}$, $L_{y'}$ and $T_c$ generate adequate posteriors), 
DSI-ESMDA may suffer the inherent history matching problem of making poor predictions despite good fit to past data.
%
The ensemble posterior narrows around a curve that is not representative of the reservoir.

The inherent problem of history matching may be impossible to solve, but it may be mitigated by allowing the posterior to spread more.
This work proposes doing so by adaptively adjusting the importance of the history mismatch when running the update iterations:
%
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{d}_j^{k+1} 
    &=
    \mathbf{d}_j^k + (\mathbf{R} \circ \mathbf{K}^k) 
    [
    \bm{\alpha}_j^k \circ 
    (\mathbf{d}_\text{obs} - \mathbf{d}_{h,\ j}^k)], 
\label{eq:iteration_gated}
    \\
    \alpha_{j,\ v}^k 
    &= \mathrm{erf}
    \left(
        \frac{1}{\lambda \sqrt{2\sigma_v^2}} 
        |(\mathbf{d}_\text{obs} - \mathbf{d}_{h,\ j}^k)_v|
    \right)
\label{eq:alpha_lambda}
\end{align}
%
The equation above is based on the work of \citeonline{Fortaleza:2022}. 
%
Statistically, the gating coefficient $\alpha^k_{j,v}\in[0, 1)$ (see \cref{fig:erf}) is the probability that 
the prediction of datum $v$ of realization $j$ reflects the same direction of its target value 
given the uncertainty in the data.
%
A detailed derivation of \cref{eq:alpha_lambda} is provided in \cref{chap:deriving-alpha}.

The gating recovers variance loss in the posterior ensemble by diminishing Kalman-like updates on reasonably matched data.
As \cref{fig:alpha} shows, the smaller the mismatch of a measurement relative to the noise at that datum, 
the smaller the coefficients, and thus the smaller the update.
Constant $\lambda\geq 1$ inflates the deviation, further softening the Kalman-like update.
At the extreme ($\lambda\to\infty$ or $\mathbf{d}_\text{obs}=\mathbf{d}_{h,\ j}^k$), $\bm{\alpha}_j^k = \mathbf{0}$ and the DSI update is fully undone.

\begin{note}[Noise modeling.]
    Technically speaking, this work will apply the standard DSI-ESMDA and the proposed algorithm 
    on normalized data vectors.
    %
    As \cref{sec:evaluation-workflow-parameters} clarifies, 
    this is done to facilitate noise injection and modeling,
    and does not affect the validity of the theory and formulas presented in this chapter.
\end{note}

The Kalman-like update in \cref{eq:iteration_dsiesmda,eq:iteration_gated} 
acts on the entirety of the realization data vector by projecting the history match correction 
through the cross-covariance of data in the history period and data in the forecast period.
%
That being the case, the quality of the update is heavily dependent on the quality of the prior ensemble;
a prior not being representative of the reference, 
or a history period with too little data,
are significant obstacles in the pursuit of a well adjusted posterior.
