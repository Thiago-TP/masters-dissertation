You are providing sufficient detail that others can draw their own inferences and construct their own explanations. 
Think of it as presenting the case for a jury.
That means that an empirical discussion should:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Tell the reader how the data was collected, with reference to the methods chapter/section
    \item Tell them how they can access it if they wanted to replicate your study
    \item Discuss what the results look like (using visual aids, such as tables, diagrams, graphs and so on)
    \item Provide rich summaries of the findings
    \item Discuss the gaps in the findings and analysis
    \item Analyse the results
    \item Discuss the implications of your findings
    \item Discuss the limitations of the findings
\end{itemize}

When it comes to writing your discussion chapter, you can start by writing a few sentences that summarise the most important results. One danger when writing discussion sections is that they can be too wordy, offer too much interpretation and lack a clear structure. To avoid this, you should make sure that every element of your discussion section addresses one of the following questions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item What are the relationships between observations? (The mud-map you developed earlier will help here.)
    \item Are there any trends and generalisations amongst the results? Are there any exceptions to these?
    \item What are the causes of, or mechanisms behind, the underlying patterns you have uncovered?
    \item Do your results agree or disagree with previous work?
    \item How do your findings relate to the theoretical framework you developed, if applicable?
    \item How do the findings relate to the hypotheses you developed, if applicable?
    \item What other explanations could there be for your results? This issue is more pertinent if you are engaging in theory creation/inductive reasoning.
    \item What do we now know as a result of your research that we didn’t know before?
    \item What is the significance of these findings?
    \item Why should we care about the findings?
\end{itemize}
%
When your discussion chapter is finished, your reader should be able to answer the following questions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item How do the findings relate to the theory and methods discussed previously?
    \item Why you have reached particular conclusions?
    \item How do your findings relate to the gaps in the literature you identified earlier?
    \item What implications do the findings have for the discipline and for existing understanding?
    \item How do the findings relate to your research questions/aims and objectives?
\end{itemize}
