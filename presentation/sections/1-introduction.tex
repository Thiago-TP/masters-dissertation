\begin{frame}{Introduction}{The inverse problem}
    To understand a reservoir, that is to say,
    to ascertain its distributions of relevant petrophysical parameters
    given its history, is called the \emph{inverse problem}
    \cite{Oliver:2011}.
    %
    \vfill
    %
    Reservoirs are approximated as gridblocks imbued with petrophysical properties.

    These values are adjusted in order to match observed data in a process called \emph{history matching}.
    %
    \vfill
    %
    However, even the most efficient history matching algorithms
    \cite{Evensen:1994,Evensen:1996,Skjervheim:2011,Emerick:2013} 
    are computationally expensive (i.e., slow)
    since they require several subsurface simulations.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Introduction}{Data-Space}
    \textbf{Data-Space Inversion (DSI)}
    \cite{Sun:2017}
    updates the predictions of reservoir model realizations directly,
    doing away with sluggish model updates.
    %
    \vfill
    %
    \textbf{Ensemble Smoother with Multiple Data Assimilation (ESMDA)}
    \cite{Emerick:2013},
    a seminal Model-Space algorithm, has been adapted to DSI,
    resulting in \textbf{DSI-ESMDA} \cite{Lima:2020}.
    %
    \vfill
    %
    Despite recent advances
    \cite{Jiang:2021,Xiao:2023,Jiang:2024}, 
    DSI-ESMDA remains suscetible to excessive variance loss.
    This work proposes \textbf{Gated DSI-ESMDA}, 
    an extension of DSI-ESMDA with a gating mechanism for variance recovery.
